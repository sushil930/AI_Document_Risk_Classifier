COMMERCIAL & OPERATIONAL INSTRUMENT: Service Continuity, Third-Party Operational Risk, and Escalation Charter
Instrument No.: DG-OPE-2026-0265 | Effective as of 2026-09-11 | Version 1.2
Between: Cobalt Health Data Exchange, Inc. and Cascade Municipal Utilities Authority
================================================================================


1. OPERATIONAL SCOPE, OBJECTIVES, AND GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK
1.1 Purpose. This Service Continuity, Third-Party Operational Risk, and Escalation Charter ("Operational Policy") defines the operational resilience, access governance, acceptable use, change management, and escalation procedures maintained by Cobalt Health Data Exchange, Inc. ("Operator") supporting mission-critical workflows for Cascade Municipal Utilities Authority as of 2026-09-11.
1.2 Covered Systems and Personnel. This Operational Policy applies to all production cloud clusters, administrative bastion hosts, CI/CD deployment pipelines, customer support tooling, and all employees, contractors, and operational personnel acting on Operator's behalf.
1.3 Operational Ownership. The Vice President of Infrastructure Operations and the Enterprise Risk Committee oversee adherence to the controls and escalation thresholds established herein.

2. CHANGE MANAGEMENT, MONITORING, AND ACCEPTABLE USE BASELINES
2.1 Production Change Control. Standard infrastructure and application releases must pass automated integration testing, peer code review, and staging validation prior to production deployment.
2.2 Acceptable Use Enforcement. Users and integrated systems are prohibited from introducing malicious code, bypassing rate limiters, conducting unauthorized load testing, or interfering with multi-tenant workload scheduling.
2.3 Continuous Telemetry. Operator maintains 24x7x365 automated health checks across compute nodes, message queues, and storage arrays with real-time alerting to the Network Operations Center (NOC).

3. EXCEPTION WAIVERS, FAILOVER TARGETS, AND OPERATIONAL CONTROLS
3.1 Specific Operational Governance Rule. Departmental directors may grant temporary operational policy exceptions—including waivers of peer change review or multi-factor authentication on legacy batch servers—for up to ninety (90) days with self-renewing extensions if documented in an internal ticketing queue, without mandatory Risk Committee sign-off.
3.2 Standard Escalation Channels. Operational disruptions are triaged by the central operations desk according to impact tier and routed to the appropriate engineering squad for remediation.
3.3 Maintenance Windows. Routine infrastructure patching occurs during weekly maintenance windows, with emergency hotfixes deployed as determined by the on-call engineering lead.

4. POLICY ADMINISTRATION AND PERIODIC UPDATES
4.1 Administrative Custody. The Infrastructure Operations team maintains the operational runbooks associated with this Operational Policy and updates procedures as platform topology evolves.
4.2 Customer Notification. Material changes to customer-facing operational workflows are communicated through release notes posted to the customer portal.
